CASE STUDY · HAIRCARE · ILLUSTRATIVE SCENARIO

Haircare Routine and Range Extension: from buyer brief to launch plan

An illustrative range-extension scenario showing how shampoo, conditioner, mask, scalp care and leave-in formats can be organized into distinct routine roles instead of competing product cards.

Scenario status

This example explains a planning framework. It does not identify a customer, confirm a completed order or report sales, performance or delivery results. Product scope, manufacturing feasibility and evidence are assessed per project.

Example project brief

A brand has an established wash product and wants to extend its offer. The key planning task is to make each new item easy to understand: rinse-off versus leave-on, scalp versus lengths, daily use versus occasional treatment, and styling preparation versus care.

Cleanse

Define the wash experience, cleansing preference, fragrance direction and target hair/scalp profile. Confirm positioning stays within cosmetic scope.

Condition and treat

Give conditioner and mask different use occasions and textures. Clarify where each is applied, how long it is used and how it fits after shampoo.

Leave-on care

Separate scalp serum, hair oil and leave-in milk by application area, finish and routine step. Select dispensing that supports controlled, hygienic use.

Project pathway

1 · Routine map

Map the current assortment and identify missing roles using buyer, retailer and search research for the intended market. Do not assume every customer needs every step.

2 · Formula brief

Specify hair profile, rinse-off or leave-on use, wet or dry application, sensory finish, fragrance, residue tolerance and compatibility requirements. Agree which observations will be judged during sampling.

3 · Ingredient and claim review

Review ingredient identity, intended function, formula compatibility, stability and evidence for the finished product. Claims about growth, loss or scalp conditions need especially careful market-specific review; avoid medical promises and guaranteed outcomes.

4 · Pack and dispensing

Compare bottle, pump, tube and jar formats with viscosity, shower handling, dose control, leakage and transport. Confirm the closure and material are appropriate for the final formula.

5 · Sample protocol

Test wash products under consistent water, dose and routine conditions. Record cleansing feel, detangling, rinse, combing and finish as sensory feedback; keep efficacy claims separate unless suitable evidence supports them.

6 · Range launch

Build clear product naming, routine instructions, cross-sell logic and FAQ content. Track real customer questions and returns to inform the next product rather than adding formats without a defined role.

Information the buyer should prepare

  • Destination markets, sales channels and intended customer.
  • Product role, reference formats and the reason each reference matters.
  • Preferred texture, fragrance, package, artwork requirements and estimated quantity.
  • Proposed claims, supporting evidence and any required local review.
  • Sample approval criteria, decision owners and indicative launch sequence.

Clear inputs help the project team separate feasibility questions from commercial preferences. Final formula, packaging, documentation, manufacturing arrangements and quote terms remain subject to written project review.

Frequently asked questions

How do I avoid overlap between a hair mask and conditioner?

Define frequency, application area, texture and routine function in the brief, then ensure labels and product pages explain the difference clearly.

Can a scalp-care page claim hair growth?

That depends on the exact formula, evidence and laws in every destination market. Treat growth language as a claim requiring specialist review; do not publish a medical or guaranteed outcome without appropriate substantiation and authorization.

What details help select a haircare package?

Formula viscosity, dose, wet-hand use, application area, travel needs, material compatibility and label space should all be included.
